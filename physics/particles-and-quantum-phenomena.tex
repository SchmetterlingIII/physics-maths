\section{Classes}
\subsection{Leptons}
Leptons are not made of constituent particles and have a $-1$ charge. The most massive is muon ($\mu$) but the most prevalent is the electron ($e$)

\subsection{Hadrons}
Hadrons are made of quarks. 

Up quarks have a charge of $+\frac{1}{3}e$

Down quarks have a charge of $-\frac{2}{3}e$

Strange quarks have a quality, \textit{a flavour}, which is strangeness. Itself has a strangeness value of $-1$ and charge of $-\frac{1}{3}e$.

\subsubsection{Baryons}
Protons and neutrons are examples of baryons which consist of three quarks, that have an integer value of charge. % why is this the case?
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Protons}: \textit{$uud$, $+1e$}
    \item \textbf{Neutrons}: \textit{$udd$, $0e$}
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Mesons}
These are a pair of quarks.

\textbf{Kaons} are a type of meson that consists of a pair of quarks but has a strangeness value associated with it as well. The sum of charges must result in a discrete value from the set $\{-1, 0, 1\}$.
